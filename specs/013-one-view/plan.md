# Implementation Plan: One View, Four Regions

**Feature**: `013-one-view` | **Spec**: [`spec.md`](./spec.md)
**Date**: 2026-09-07 | **Depends on**: 007 (the row), 008 (the footprint), 010 (counterfactuals)
**Milestone**: the first beat of SRD-v2. It opens the surface beats and it moves no number.

## Summary

The application becomes one non-scrolling viewport of four regions — controls left, the
horizon row centre, each panel's scores in that panel's own column beneath it, the last
selection right. Nothing new is computed. The claim that nothing new is computed is the
expensive part, and it is bought first: a gate that digests every computed quantity of the
recorded case and refuses any change to any of them, landed and watched failing before the
first element moves.

## The order this beat is built in, and why it is not negotiable

FR-001 says the gate lands first. That is not a preference about test-first discipline. A
1,301-line component whose presentation and computation have never been separated is about
to be taken apart; without a committed record of what it currently computes, "the refactor
changed no numbers" would be an assertion by the person who did the refactoring, which is
the one witness this project never accepts.

1. **G-07 lands green over the tree and red over a planted coefficient.** No layout file is
   touched in that commit.
2. Then the regions, then the scores in their columns, then the detail region, then the
   floor. G-07 runs on every one of those commits.

## Where the record lives, and why not in `data/`

The record is `scripts/gates/records/surface-invariance.json`: one entry per computed
quantity, its digest, and the sentence naming what produced it.

`data/` is for artefacts converted from upstream by `data/scripts/convert.py` and held by
G-01; putting a digest of *this repository's own code* beside them would invite the two to be
regenerated by the same command, which they must not be — G-01 asks whether the conversion
still produces the committed bytes, and G-07 asks whether the code still produces the
committed figures. They fail for different reasons and are re-recorded by different people at
different times. The record therefore sits with the gate that owns it, inside an existing
top-level directory. No new top-level directory is proposed.

## What is digested, and what that exposes

The producer walks the recorded case from the declared seed through every module that
computes anything, and digests each result:

| Quantity | Producer |
|---|---|
| the declared configuration | `digestOf(config)` |
| initialisation, and state bytes at step 0 and after the declared advance | `initialiseFromTruth`, `Run.advance`, `stateBytes` |
| published results and their invariants | `publishResults` |
| every instrument's observations | `sampleSurface`, `sampleXbtDrops`, `argoObservations` |
| the analysis field, its attribution weights, its used and excluded sets | `analyse` |
| each declared horizon's forecast field | `runForecast` |
| the departure brief | `departureBrief` |
| each declared horizon's score, both skills and all three errors | `score` |
| the observation footprint a panel reads | `footprintOf` |

The last two rows are where the entanglement the spec expects becomes visible, and both are
recorded as findings rather than resolved quietly:

- **The per-horizon scores have no headless producer.** `HorizonRow.scoreAll` computes every
  panel's `Score` inside a React component and keeps it in component state, so today the only
  way to obtain the figures the surface shows is to render the surface. The gate re-derives
  them from `runForecast` and `score`; the beat then lifts `scoreAll` onto exactly that
  derivation, and the gate is what proves the lift changed nothing. This is finding 2 of the
  spec — *a computation that happens because something is drawn* — and it is the reason the
  gate had to come first rather than the reason it can be skipped.
- **The footprint is built in order to be drawn.** `footprintFor` runs three times per render
  from `App.tsx`, inline in JSX and uncached; and because the prop it hands the row is a fresh
  object each render, `HorizonRow`'s own `useMemo` around `markersFrom` never hits, so `marksOf`
  runs at least seven times more. Same finding, second instance. (An earlier reading of this
  plan said `HorizonRow` constructs a fourth footprint; it does not — it imports only the
  derivations.)

## What the record cannot hold, stated before it is relied on

Writing G-07 found three limits, and a gate whose limits are not written down is trusted for
more than it holds.

- **The `score.*` and `briefScore.*` entries are a digest of a transcription.** `scoreAll` lives
  inside a React callback, so the gate re-derives the per-horizon scores by copying its body.
  Until T020 lifts `scoreAll` onto that same derivation and both call one producer, a divergence
  between what the gate digests and what the row draws would be invisible to the gate rather than
  caught by it. T020 is therefore not an optimisation; it is what makes seventeen of the entries
  mean what they say.
- **Seven of the forty-one entries cannot detect a change to the analysis.** `quaysideOffsetHours`
  is zero, so `departureBrief` analyses at the run's start with nothing sampled: the brief is the
  prior blend of background and climatology, exactly as `forecast.ts` says, and no analysis
  coefficient can move it. The record is weaker than its count suggests, and it says so here
  rather than in a footnote nobody reads.
- **Two of the walk's inputs are literals with no declaration behind them.** `ADVANCE_HOURS = 12`
  and `scoringLeadHours = 24` are numbers in a component; the gate had to copy them, so the record
  is pinned to two values no schema would refuse a change to. That is finding 3 of this beat, and
  it is recorded rather than fixed.

## Decisions this plan makes

- **The grid is CSS grid with four named areas, and the column count comes from
  `horizons.leadHours.length`** (FR-012), handed to the stylesheet as a custom property the
  way beat 007 hands it the row geometry. A configuration with five or seven horizons lays
  out without a code change.
- **The scores are not a table.** Each panel's figures render inside that panel's grid
  column, in a `scores` row that shares the grid's column track with the row above it, so the
  alignment is structural rather than arranged. The existing `score-panel` section is removed,
  not hidden — FR-006 says no scores table exists elsewhere.
- **The narrative sections stay for now.** FR-42 is beat 014's, and deleting them here would
  put this beat in the business of moving prose. They move behind the controls region as
  reachable disclosures until 014 takes them off the application entirely; what this beat
  removes from the first viewport is their claim on vertical space.
- **The floor is measured, not chosen** (FR-010, and SRD-v2 §9). A Playwright measurement
  walks the viewport down until a panel falls below `minimumPanelWidthPx` or a region's
  content exceeds its box, and prints the width and height it found. Those two figures are
  then declared in `presentation` as `minimumViewportWidthPx` and `minimumViewportHeightPx`,
  refused by the schema if they cannot hold the declared horizons, and read by both the
  application and the tests. No literal appears in a component or in CSS.
- **Below the floor the application does not shrink the row.** It states the size it needs as
  a `Declared` figure and offers the single-panel presentation, which is FR-49's enlargement
  reached as a fallback. 015 makes enlargement a proper selection; this beat needs only that
  the fallback is operable, and says so.
- **Internal scroll is declared, not accidental.** A region that may scroll within itself
  carries a `data-scrolls` attribute, and the no-scroll test asserts that every element with a
  scrolling overflow is one of the declared ones. An undeclared scrollbar fails the test by
  name.

## What the regions cost, measured

Two figures came out of building them, and both change a declared value.

- **The reference width no longer holds the row.** Six panels at the declared minimum need
  1 190 px; the controls and detail columns plus the gutter need 848 more. `1400` was declared in
  beat 007 for a row that had the whole window, and the measured minimum for four regions is
  **2 038**. The centre must not answer that by scrolling horizontally — that is beat 007's own
  finding coming back through a different door, and it is what FR-43 exists to answer instead. So
  the floor is declared at the measured figure and below it the application says the size it needs.
- **T040 measured both figures from the built layout: 2 038 × 682 CSS px.** The width is the
  arithmetic above and agrees with it to the pixel, because it is a sum of declared boxes. The
  height was not predictable and was not predicted: the row with its labels and its six score
  statements is 634 px tall at that width, and the page gutter is the other 48. The height is
  measured at the *floor's own width* rather than at a roomy one, because a score statement is a
  sentence that wraps in its column and a wider panel is a shorter score.
- **`referenceViewportWidthPx` is raised from 1400 to 2038.** It is the width the documentation
  photographs the application at, and photographing it at a width where the centre scrolled would
  be publishing the fault. The schema now refuses a reference below the floor.
- **The two column widths are declared rather than derived.** `controlsWidthPx` and
  `detailWidthPx` were left optional and absent so that G-07's `configuration` digest would not
  move; that is the gate shaping the design instead of holding it. They are required, they are
  390 each — two panels and the gap between them, which is what the detail region needs to draw a
  profile editor whole — and `schemaVersion` goes to 7 with them.
- **G-07's first quantity is a digest of the whole validated configuration**, so declaring any
  presentation figure — a column width, the floor itself — moves a recorded digest although
  nothing computed moves. That is a real cost of the gate and it is stated rather than worked
  around: the declared values are declared, `configuration` is re-recorded once, and the commit
  says which quantity moved and why. A gate that made declaring a number expensive enough to avoid
  would be shaping the design instead of holding it.

**Result: PASS.** No Complexity Tracking entry.

## Risks this plan accepts

- **The walkthrough of beat 011 resolves DOM anchors by test id.** Moving every section
  breaks it. It is updated in the same commit as the regions, and its shell test is the
  detector.
- **`fullPage: true` screenshots stop meaning anything** once the page is one viewport. The
  screenshot suite moves to the declared floor and the declared reference width.
- **The domain choice of SRD-v1 FR-11 has no control today** — `App.tsx` reads
  `domains.defaultId` and nothing offers the second domain. FR-004 puts it in the controls
  region, so this beat builds it. It changes no computed quantity for the recorded case,
  which is the default domain, and the gate holds that.

## Measured

- **5 757 px to 900 px.** At the width declared when the beat began, the application was 6.4
  vertical screens of ten stacked sections in a 46-rem prose column. It is now one viewport in
  every state it can reach: loaded, row-unbuilt, row-built, scored, cell-selected, over-budget
  and configuration-failure, on both axes (SC-001).
- **The floor is 2 038 x 682 CSS pixels**, measured from the built layout rather than chosen, and
  tight: a panel is *at* its declared 190 px and the centre has no spare height, so one pixel
  less is one pixel too few. `referenceViewportWidthPx` rose from 1 400 to meet it.
- **Four declared internal scrollers**, and the test found a fifth that nobody declared: every
  field's accessible mark list was absolutely positioned inside an unpositioned `figure`, so its
  containing block was the viewport. It escaped every scroll container and lengthened the page by
  33 px.
- **G-07 held the whole beat.** Forty-one quantities, forty of them byte-identical from the first
  commit to the last across a rewrite of `App.tsx`, `HorizonRow.tsx` and `Panel.tsx`. One moved:
  `configuration`, because presentation figures that compute nothing are inside the digest of the
  validated configuration, and this beat declared four of them. Re-recorded once, with both
  digests in the commit message.
- **256 headless tests, 54 shell tests, 17 screenshots, eight gates.**

## What the plan said, and what landed instead

- **The narrative did not move behind the controls' disclosures as prose.** The run, the declared
  values, the instruments, the truth record, the manifest and the deferrals did; the introductory
  matter and the blog are still the site's, waiting for beat 014, which is where FR-42 puts them.
- **The skill curve became a disclosure rather than a band across the scores' tracks.** Stretched
  over six columns it was some 600 px tall, and the requirement is that the figures show the decay
  without a curve being plotted. The curve is an aid to reading them, so it is offered rather than
  spent on.
- **A control was built that the plan expected to move.** SRD-v1 FR-11 declares two domains and
  nothing on the surface had ever offered the second, so building the picker was the first time
  anyone ran it -- and found it cannot be run. That is question 4.
- **The floor was worse than the plan guessed and is stated rather than softened.** A 13-inch
  laptop is below it. The honest answer was already in SRD-v2 FR-43: this instrument wants width,
  and a no-scroll requirement without a smallest-case answer is an unfinished requirement.
